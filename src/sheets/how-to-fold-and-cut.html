How to Fold & Cut

cut along dashed
fold on dotted
Dashed lineScissors line. Cut all the way through.
Dotted lineFold line. Crease before or after cutting, as noted on each sheet.
Thin grey ruleWrite-in line. Use a pencil first if you like to change your mind.
  1. Print this kit on US Letter paper at 100% scale ("Actual size" in your print dialog), single-sided.
  2. Plain-style sheets look sharpest on regular paper; waterproof bags and canisters pair well with clear packing tape over the label.
  3. Cut along every dashed line. A paper trimmer is faster; scissors are fine.
  4. Where a piece has a dotted line, fold it in half and crease firmly (run a thumbnail or spoon along the fold).
  5. Fold-over jar and canister labels wrap around the jar rim or lid edge; tape the back so they stay put.

Fold-over label (jars & canisters)

FLOUR
contents & date lines on the back

Cut around the dashed border, fold on the dotted line, and hang the fold over the jar rim. Front shows the name; back holds the date you refilled it.

Fold-in-half card (spice blends & mixes)

TACO BLEND — recipe below

Cut around the dashed border, fold on the dotted line so the recipe hides inside, and store the card in the jar or spice drawer. Long-life details stay with the blend.

Kitchen-friendly tips

  • Bulk-print a spare set before you need it; refills are already included as write-in cards.
  • Pencil for anything dated, so labels survive menu changes.
  • Hang the Use-Within Cheat Chart inside a cabinet door where you decant leftovers.
  • The Shelf Category Map works best taped at eye level inside the pantry door; mark zones "new behind old."
  • All guidelines on these sheets are friendly rules of thumb, not gospel — when in doubt, look, smell, and toss.
